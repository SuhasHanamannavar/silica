— Die Architecture

A modern semiconductor die is a vertically stacked system of extraordinary complexity. Beneath the surface, multiple layers of patterned metal interconnect form a dense transportation network, routing signals between billions of transistors arranged in standard-cell rows. Power distribution grids, clock trees, and I/O structures complete the architecture.

— Layer Stack

A semiconductor die is composed of a complex series of stacked materials. At the foundation lies the silicon substrate, doped to create transistor channels. Above this, polysilicon gates and diffusion regions form the active devices. Multiple layers of metal interconnect route signals vertically and horizontally.

Standard Cells

Pre-characterized logic gates arranged in uniform-height rows — the fundamental building blocks of digital implementation.

I/O Structures

Perimeter circuits translating internal voltage levels to external interface standards, with ESD protection.

Clock Distribution

Balanced networks delivering the timing reference with controlled skew across billions of sequential elements.

Power Grid

Multi-layer mesh distributing VDD and VSS with controlled IR drop and electromigration margins.
