The Design Journey

01 / Workflow
01
Frontend

Specification

Architecture, performance targets, power budget, and die-area envelope defined. The contract every downstream stage is measured against.

02
Frontend

RTL Design

Behavioral description in hardware-description language — registers, datapaths, and finite state machines captured as synthesizable logic.

03
Frontend

Functional Verification

Testbenches, assertions, and coverage closure prove the RTL does what the specification requires, before a single gate is placed.

04
Frontend

Synthesis

RTL mapped to a technology library of standard cells. The design becomes a netlist of gates, sized for timing, area, and power.

05
Backend

Floorplanning

Macro blocks, I/O rings, and power domains arranged on the die. The skeleton on which every later stage is built.

06
Backend

Placement

Standard cells positioned legal-row by legal-row, optimized for wirelength, timing, and routability across the core area.

07
Backend

Routing

Metal interconnect drawn across stacked layers — signals, clock tree, and power grid connected without design-rule violation.

08
Signoff

Timing Analysis

Static timing closure across all process, voltage, and temperature corners. Setup and hold margins verified before signoff.

09
Signoff

Physical Verification

Design-rule check, layout-versus-schematic, and antenna checks confirm the drawn geometry is manufacturable and electrically correct.

10
Signoff

Tapeout Preparation

Final GDSII/OASIS database, signoff reports, and fabrication handoff package assembled for external foundry manufacture.

Eleven layers of metal, one substrate of silicon.

Like the stacked emulsion layers of a film negative, a modern chip is a vertical stack. Lower layers are dense and fine, wiring transistors together. Upper layers grow thick and wide, distributing clock and power across the die.

Each layer is patterned by photolithography, etched, and filled with copper — the same care of alignment, layer after layer, until the last metal is capped and the design is ready to leave the building.

From design to handoff.

When every check is clean and every corner is closed, Silica assembles the final package: layout database, signoff reports, and fabrication notes — everything an external foundry needs to turn drawn geometry into silicon.

Silica does not manufacture wafers. It prepares the design so that those who do, can.
